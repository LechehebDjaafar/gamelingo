# Legal and safety notes

> This is practical guidance, not legal advice. Rules differ by country, publisher and game.

## What GameLingo is for
Translating **single-player / offline** games the user **owns**, for **personal use** or as a **text-only overlay** shared with people who also own the game.

## Do
- Work on the user's own installed copy; back up before touching anything; keep every change reversible.
- Distribute only your translation (text files, fonts you may redistribute) plus installer scripts — never the game's own files, assets, executables or extracted resources.
- Check the publisher's EULA / modding policy and any official stance on fan translations. Some publishers allow them, some require permission, some forbid them.
- Prefer the engine's official or conventional mod/localization mechanism.
- Credit the original game and its developers; say clearly that the patch is unofficial.

## Don't
- Don't bypass DRM, license checks, or online anti-cheat. Don't help patch executables to defeat protections.
- Don't modify online / competitive multiplayer games: file changes can trigger bans (EasyAntiCheat, BattlEye, Vanguard, VAC-secured servers, etc.).
- Don't sell translations of someone else's game or bundle them with pirated copies.
- Don't publish extracted assets, full text dumps, or decompiled source of the game.
- Don't ship fonts without checking their license (SIL OFL fonts such as Noto, Cairo, Tajawal and IBM Plex are fine to redistribute with the license text).

## Practical risks
- Game updates and "verify integrity" will overwrite the patch.
- Machine translation can contain errors or inappropriate wording; review sensitive text.
- Modified saves/data can corrupt progress: keep save backups.
